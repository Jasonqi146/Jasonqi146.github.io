For the American Express default prediction challenge, I built a full tabular modeling pipeline around feature engineering, validation discipline, and ensemble design. The interesting part was not any single model but the way the pieces reinforced each other.

I experimented with temporal aggregations, categorical encodings, and blended gradient-boosting systems to improve both leaderboard performance and generalization. The final setup placed in the top 1.6 percent of the competition.

It was a useful reminder that strong ML systems often come from careful iteration on data representation and evaluation, not just from reaching for a more complicated architecture.
